# Write files

Every file actually sent to the instrument. These are the ground truth for what
the tip did, and they are what `make_writes_figure.py` draws.

- `campaign1/` — the operator-supervised runs: pulse-lattice templates
  (`tpl_*`), the block sequence (`block*`), the same-charge and no-charge
  rewrites (`rewrite_*`), spirals and raw XYV trajectories (`spiral*`,
  `TrajLitho_XYV*`), and the readout runs that followed them (`zoom_*`,
  `hires_*`, `retention_*`, `retraster_*`, `triadall_*`, `fineangle_*`,
  `screen_*`, `probe_area_*`, `diag_*`, `tile*`, `scanrot_*`).
- `campaign2/` — the agent-driven iterations, generated by
  `src/patterns/template_lib.py` and `gen_pulse_lattice.py`, including the
  masked UTK lattice of IT10b.

A lattice file lists one stationary site per line with its position and bias.
Sign alternates between rows, and each panel carries equal numbers of positive
and negative sites, so the voltage-time exposure is balanced. Current was not
measured, so no charge is claimed. Trajectory files (`TrajLitho_XYV*`) are
position and voltage samples along a continuous path.

The bias reversal distance in a dense trajectory is smaller than one lamellar
period, which is why a trajectory and a lattice are not the same operation even
at matched exposure. That reading came from the generator code, not from the
images; see Section 4.1 of the paper.
